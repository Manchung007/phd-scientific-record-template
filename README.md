# PhD Scientific Record Template

A free GitHub Pages template for graduate students to document their scientific thinking, organize thesis materials, prepare an academic CV, and build a public-facing research profile.

## Purpose

This template is a **living scientific record**, not merely a personal homepage. It helps a student document:

- The scientific questions they are trying to answer.
- Their hypotheses, experimental or analytical strategies, and personal contributions.
- Evidence, uncertainty, failed experiments, and next decisions.
- Literature gaps and links between published work and their own projects.
- Methods, skills, outputs, presentations, CV material, and academic profiles.

**Core rule:** Do not record only what you did. Record why you did it, what you learned, and how it changed your next decision.

## Files

- `index.html` — Research identity, core question, current project snapshot, and monthly update.
- `projects.html` — Reusable project-record framework.
- `research-log.html` — Weekly or monthly scientific-reasoning log.
- `literature.html` — Literature map and knowledge-gap framework.
- `methods.html` — Skills, instruments, analysis workflows, and method decisions.
- `outputs.html` — Publications, presentations, awards, CV, and academic-profile links.

## Start here

1. Click **Use this template** on GitHub, or fork this repository.
2. Rename the repository, for example: `firstname-lastname-scientific-record`.
3. Edit every `[PLACEHOLDER]` in the HTML files.
4. Start with `index.html`, then add one project in `projects.html` and one entry in `research-log.html`.
5. Commit changes regularly with meaningful messages, for example: `Add September research summary`.

## Publish with GitHub Pages

1. Open your repository on GitHub.
2. Go to **Settings** → **Pages**.
3. Under **Build and deployment**, choose **Deploy from a branch**.
4. Select the `main` branch and the `/(root)` folder.
5. Click **Save**.
6. GitHub will display your website URL after deployment.

## Suggested update rhythm

| Frequency | Minimum update |
|---|---|
| Weekly | One concise Research Log entry: question, observation, uncertainty, next decision. |
| Monthly | Update the project snapshot and write a synthesis of what changed in your understanding. |
| Each semester | Review your literature map, methods/skills, and project conclusions. |
| Before a milestone | Use the project records to prepare a thesis chapter, committee report, presentation, or CV. |

## Public vs private information

GitHub Pages websites are public. **Do not upload**:

- Unpublished raw data, lab notebooks, confidential figures, or manuscript drafts.
- Patent-sensitive, industry-sponsored, or collaborator-confidential information.
- Human-subject, clinical, personally identifiable, restricted, or regulated data.
- Passwords, tokens, API keys, institutional credentials, or private contact details.
- Copyrighted PDFs or materials you do not have permission to redistribute.

Keep detailed experiments, raw data, private notes, and sensitive collaborations in an approved private location: an institutional system, laboratory server, approved cloud drive, electronic lab notebook, or private repository.

A useful label for every record is: `Visibility: Private / Group-only / Public`.

## Responsible AI use

AI can help you:

- Convert your own verified notes into a clear project-log structure.
- Improve clarity and grammar without changing scientific meaning.
- Identify missing controls, alternative interpretations, or unclear causal claims.
- Draft an outline for a CV, thesis chapter, or research statement based only on information you provide.

AI must not:

- Invent data, results, citations, authors, DOIs, methods, skills, awards, or contributions.
- Replace your scientific judgement, data checking, or authorship responsibility.
- Receive confidential, unpublished, sensitive, or restricted information unless your institution and collaborators explicitly permit it.

Before public release or submission, verify every fact yourself.

## Research-record prompts

For each project, answer:

1. What exact scientific question am I trying to answer?
2. Why does this gap matter?
3. What is my current hypothesis?
4. What evidence supports or challenges it?
5. What alternative explanation remains?
6. What is the next most informative decision?

## Teaching use

This template can support a thesis-writing or research-methods course. Suggested first assignment:

1. Create a copy of the template.
2. Complete your research identity and one core scientific question.
3. Document one project with its strategy, current evidence, uncertainty, and next decision.
4. Add two key papers to the literature map.
5. Add one research-log entry.
6. Submit the repository URL privately to the instructor; do not make sensitive content public.
